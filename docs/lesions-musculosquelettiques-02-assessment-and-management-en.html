Assessment and management
A single approach for fractures, dislocations, sprains and strains
Same approach, four injuries. Mechanism, PQRST, comparative exam, CSM, then RICE, immobilization and the evacuation decision.

1
The mechanism guides everything. Direct force (fracture at the point of impact), indirect (at a distance) or twisting (sprain, spiral fracture). Type, direction and magnitude of the force: your first objective data.
2
PQRST, then comparative exam. One limb at a time, palpation along the full length of the bone, comparison with the uninjured side, active then passive range of motion. Rings and watches removed. When in doubt, treat as a fracture.
3
CSM: the finding that decides the evacuation. Circulation (distal pulse, colour, warmth, capillary refill), sensation, motion. Impaired CSM makes the evacuation urgent and justifies, for a grossly angulated long bone, gentle in-line traction. Stop if there is resistance or increasing pain.
4
RICE, and what not to do. Ice 15 to 20 minutes while monitoring CSM, rest, elevation, light non-restrictive compression. Heat and massage later, never on a contusion. Acetaminophen or NSAIDs for pain and swelling.
5
Dislocations: recognize, immobilize, do not play surgeon. Shoulder: arm held away from the body, bulge at the front; immobilize without reducing, evacuate quickly. Finger, toe, kneecap without suspected fracture: gentle reduction possible, then CSM. Hip and knee: advanced care only.
