Introduction to the chapter
Seven lessons where prevention is worth more than treatment
Why this chapter: the environment does not injure like a fall or a knife. It works over time, often silently: water that makes you sick three days later, a tick attached for 36 hours, a venom that spreads, cold water that takes away your motor control in ten minutes. Here, the right decision is made before the incident.
1
Three blocks: prevent, bites and stings, water. Drinking water and lightning are matters of preparation and reading the terrain. Bites and stings (animals, snakes, arachnids, mosquitoes) share the same logic: wound, infection, venom or transmitted disease. Submersion closes the module with a rescue rule.
2
Which type of shock? Hypovolemic for diarrhea and hemotoxic envenomations, respiratory or obstructive for submersion and some neurotoxic venoms, cardiogenic for lightning, distributive for an allergic reaction to a sting. Ask yourself the question in every lesson.
3
Find out before you leave. Animals, insects, snakes, diseases and vaccines of the region visited. What you do not know before departure, you will not be able to improvise on site. The travel clinic is part of the emergency plan.
4
Rescuer safety, again. Lightning, moving water, suspicious animal: several lessons in this module begin with a decision about your own exposure. A second patient does not save the first.
5
In class: cold, heat, the body's water. Hypothermia, frostbite, heat stroke, dehydration, hyponatremia. These emergencies are practised in person, with the hypothermia wrap and the scenarios.
