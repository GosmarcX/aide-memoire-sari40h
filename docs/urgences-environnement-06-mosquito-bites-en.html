Mosquito bites
Transmitted diseases and insect repellents
Mosquitoes are the leading disease vectors in the world. The infections look alike at first: fever, headache, muscle pain, fatigue. Most often there is no specific treatment; prevention and prompt consultation make the difference.
DiseaseWhereKey points
West Nile virusSeveral continents, including North AmericaFlu-like, sometimes encephalitis
MalariaTropical areasAny fever on return = malaria until proven otherwise; coma, organ failure
Dengue, chikungunya, ZikaAedes mosquitoes, tropical areasFever, pain, rash; hemorrhagic dengue; Zika dangerous in pregnancy
Yellow feverAfrica, South AmericaLiver damage, hemorrhage; the vaccine is the prevention
Figure 2. The main mosquito-borne diseases.
1
Before departure: the travel clinic. Vaccines, preventive medications (malaria), risks specific to the region. It is not the first aider's job to improvise prophylaxis on site.
2
Any fever after a tropical stay is an emergency until proven otherwise. Symptoms may appear several days after exposure. Prompt medical consultation; in the meantime, supportive care: hydration, rest, symptom treatment.
3
The repellents that work. DEET (very effective, sometimes irritating; follow the doses, especially in children, watch for dermatitis, hives, rare neurological effects) and icaridin (not before 6 months of age). Also: permethrin on clothing (toxic to animals, caution in young children), oil of lemon eucalyptus, metofluthrin. Citronella and natural products are not very effective.
4
Physical measures are essential. Long, light-coloured clothing, pants tucked into socks, mosquito net, no perfume, avoid going out at night. In high-risk areas, combine everything.
5
Recognizing a reaction that is no longer local. Most bites are benign. Significant swelling, fever, rash, symptoms developing over the following days: consult.
