Splints
The immobilization principles you will apply in class
Immobilizing means preventing unnecessary movement to relieve pain, relax the muscles, protect vessels and nerves, and keep a closed fracture from becoming open during transport. After any immobilization: CSM.

Figure 3. Four steps, in this order, whatever the splint.
1
What to immobilize: the neighbours rule. Fractured bone: the joint above and the joint below. Injured joint: the bone above and the bone below. For the lower limbs, the uninjured leg can serve as support; the knee stays flexed 5 to 10 degrees with padding under the hollow.
2
Three families of splints, plus improvisation. Soft (sleeping bag, clothing, foam), rigid (wood, plastic, padded aluminum such as the SAM Splint), anatomical (finger to finger, leg to leg). Improvised: stick, paddle, pack frame, sleeping pad. The traction splint is reserved for the femur and contraindicated for joints.
3
The configurations to know. Single, double, sugar-tong (immobilization in both planes), simple and tubular sling, figure-eight bandage for the clavicle. In class, the list is limited to: clavicle, humerus, elbow, forearm, wrist, knee, ankle, foot, lower leg.
4
Firm, never restrictive. Adhesive tape keeps its compression; elastic bandages loosen or tighten: do not overtighten them. Leave an extremity accessible for CSM. In prolonged immobilization, release periodically.
5
In cold weather, the splint cools the limb. An immobilized patient produces less heat and circulation slows in the limb: risk of hypothermia and frostbite. Insulate the limb, insulate the patient, monitor the colour and warmth of the extremities.
