The musculoskeletal system
Understanding the structures to recognize what is injured
The locomotor system is the skeleton and the musculature: bones, joints, ligaments, cartilage, muscles and tendons. Together, they give the body its shape, protect the organs, and produce movement and heat.
StructureTypical injuryWhat gives it away
BoneClosed or open fractureLocalized pain, deformity, loss of function, crepitus
JointDislocationDeformity, locked joint, loss of mobility
LigamentSprainPain on movement, swelling, instability
Muscle and tendonStrain, tendinitis, crampPain on contraction, stiffness, burning at the start of exertion
Figure 1. Four structures, four typical injuries, and what gives them away on examination.
1
Bones: five types, two skeletons. Long, short, flat, irregular, sesamoid. The axial skeleton protects (skull, spine, rib cage); the appendicular skeleton moves (limbs, girdles). It takes considerable force to fracture a bone: look for the mechanism.
2
Mobile joints: the most mobile are the least stable. Cartilage, capsule, synovial fluid, ligaments. Shoulder, kneecap and fingers are the most frequent dislocations. Hinge (knee, elbow): a single plane; ball-and-socket (hip, shoulder): all planes.
3
Ligaments limit range of motion. A forced movement beyond the normal range stretches or tears the ligament: that is a sprain. Pushed further, it displaces the joint: that is a dislocation.
4
Muscles and tendons pull, they do not push. Muscle is elastic and highly vascularized; it heals well. Tendon is rich in nerves (intense pain) but poor in vessels (slow healing). Strain: overstretching. Tendinitis: repeated overload.
5
The vocabulary of movement describes the injury. Flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, rotation, inversion and eversion. "Fall on a hyperextended hand" already points to the structure involved.
