Introduction to the chapter
Three lessons to understand, decide and immobilize
Why this chapter: in the field, you will never see the bone. You will see pain, deformity, loss of function. Your job is to decide whether you can let the patient walk, whether to immobilize, and how fast to evacuate.
1
Four injuries to recognize. Fracture (the bone), dislocation (the joint), sprain (the ligament), strain (the muscle or tendon). Their signs often look alike; when in doubt, treat as a fracture.
2
One rule before all others: CSM. Circulation, sensation, motion distal to the injury, before and after every manoeuvre, then at regular intervals. Impaired CSM changes the evacuation priority.
3
RICE for almost everything. Rest, ice, compression, elevation. This is the management common to all four injuries once the injury has been stabilized.
4
Reasoning online, hands-on in class. The Field Manual (pages 128 to 154) is your reference tool for immobilization: look at the picture, reproduce it. In class, each participant immobilizes at least one bone and one joint with their own equipment.
5
Three scenarios in the in-person module. Knee or ankle sprain, muscle strain, muscle cramps, in addition to the fracture. Prepare by reviewing the signs and management of each.
