04
Snakebites
Recognize, slow the venom, evacuate
Not all bites are venomous, but when venom is injected, its effects can be serious and rapid. The only specific treatment is antivenom, in a hospital setting. Your role: slow the spread, monitor, evacuate.
1
Three families, two types of venom. Vipers (rattlesnakes, vipers): mainly hemotoxic venom, pain, swelling, bleeding. Elapids (cobras, coral snakes): neurotoxic venom, weakness, vision and swallowing problems, respiratory paralysis. Colubrids: mostly harmless. Note what you saw, without chasing the animal.
2
Calm, lie down, immobilize below heart level. Limit all movement of the patient and the limb. Exertion speeds the spread of venom. Remove rings and bracelets before swelling sets in.
3
Mark and time. Trace the edge of the swelling on the skin with a pen every 15 minutes, noting the time. Watch for new signs: some venoms act quickly, others produce delayed symptoms.
4
Pressure immobilization: for neurotoxic venoms only. Compression bandage and immobilization of the limb to slow the spread. Indicated for elapids; contraindicated for some vipers such as rattlesnakes, where it worsens local tissue damage. If unsure of the species, do not apply it.
5
Evacuate by carrying; never a tourniquet or suction. Litter transport preferably; if walking is unavoidable to evacuate quickly, limit exertion to the strict minimum. Tourniquets, incision, suction and ice make things worse. Even without symptoms, medical assessment is mandatory: antivenom is species-specific and is given under monitoring.
