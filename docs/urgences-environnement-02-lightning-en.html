Lightning
An unpredictable phenomenon: anticipate, protect yourself, resuscitate
Ten percent of lightning flashes reach the ground, and those are the ones that injure, directly or by spreading through the environment. Lightning follows the path of least resistance, at up to 300,000 amperes. It cannot be predicted; you read the sky and act beforehand.
1
Protecting yourself when a storm approaches. Get off the water and away from ridges, avoid isolated trees and tall objects, spread out among small trees, crouch on an insulating object (life jacket, pack), remove metal objects when possible, watch for falling trees.
2
The injury that kills: cardiorespiratory arrest. About 30% of deaths. Direct cardiac arrest, or respiratory paralysis leading to hypoxia and then secondary arrest. Immediate CPR saves lives in lightning victims: start without delay, even if several patients appear lifeless.
3
Neurological injuries are the rule. Loss of consciousness in 70% of victims, confusion, amnesia, temporary paralysis. In severe cases, brain injury and permanent sequelae. Reassess consciousness and motor function at regular intervals.
4
Burns and trauma: look for the entry and exit points. Burns are often superficial, sometimes in branching patterns (Lichtenberg figures), deeper under clothing or heated metal. Any organ located between the entry and exit points may have been affected. The shock wave can throw the patient: blunt trauma, eardrums, lungs, eyes.
5
After the strike: monitor and evacuate as a precaution. A patient who regains consciousness without apparent trauma remains at risk of secondary complications (cardiac, neurological). Precautionary medical assessment, complete PAS, spinal precautions if thrown.
