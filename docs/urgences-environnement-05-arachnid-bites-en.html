Arachnid bites
Spiders, scorpions and ticks
Eight legs, no antennae, no wings. In North America, most spiders and scorpions pose no serious danger. Two spiders, one scorpion and one tick-borne disease are worth knowing.
1
Brown recluse: a bite that digs in. Light brown, violin marking, dark places, active at night. Two small marks, then a pale, painful area, sometimes necrosis and healing over several months. In children: nausea, headache, vomiting, cramps.
2
Black widow: a venom that mimics a heart attack. Glossy black, red hourglass, only the female is dangerous. Neurotoxic venom: muscle cramps, fatigue, dizziness, heavy sweating, nausea; locally, redness and mild swelling. Resolves in 48 to 72 hours, possible complications in children, older adults or immunosuppressed people.
3
Scorpions: like a bee sting, with one exception. Mainly in the North American Southwest. Local pain, redness, inflammation. The Arizona bark scorpion can cause neuromuscular effects and respiratory distress.
4
Common management and prevention. Clean and disinfect, cold for pain, hydration, analgesic, tetanus status checked, monitoring. Evacuation if systemic symptoms or allergic reaction. Children: faster and more severe progression. Shake out boots and clothing, close the tent, gloves for wood and rocks.
5
Ticks and Lyme disease: remove quickly, monitor for 30 days. Transmission after 24 to 36 hours of attachment. Fine-tipped tweezers close to the skin, slow steady pull without crushing, wash with soap and water, keep the tick, note the date and location. Early signs: fatigue, fever, body aches, erythema migrans (expanding red patch). Preventive treatment depending on region, attachment time, appearance and delay: a medical decision.
