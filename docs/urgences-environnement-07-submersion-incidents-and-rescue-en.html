Submersion incidents and rescue
Analyze, decide safely, act
Drowning is respiratory impairment from immersion or submersion. It leads to lack of oxygen, respiratory arrest and then cardiac arrest. It can be fatal or non-fatal, with or without sequelae, and complications can occur hours later.

Figure 3. The ladder approach: the first three steps are defensive, the last is offensive.
1
Defensive first, offensive never without training. Talk, throw, reach: what any first aider can do from shore. Wade, row, swim: reserved for trained people, and swimming is a last resort even for them. If you reach the patient: give them something that floats, tow them, carry them.
2
Cold water acts in three stages: 1-10-1. One minute to get your breathing under control (involuntary gasp, hyperventilation, panic, pulse and blood pressure climbing, possible arrhythmias). Ten minutes of useful movement for self-rescue. About one hour before hypothermia causes loss of consciousness.
3
Patient out of the water: ABC, CPR, vomiting. An unconscious patient who is not breathing receives CPR immediately. Expect vomiting; recovery position as soon as breathing resumes. Treat hypothermia, evacuate: respiratory complications can appear hours later.
4
What influences survival. Water temperature (cold protects the brain but causes cold shock and hypothermia; in very cold water, the odds are better), the patient's condition (children and vulnerable people are at higher risk), water quality (lung infections), speed of resuscitation and oxygenation.
5
Submersion time guides the decision. After 30 minutes: reassess resources, risks and benefits. Between 60 and 90 minutes: survival rare but possible (child, very cold water). Beyond that: the operation shifts from rescue to recovery, with minimal risk to rescuers. These decisions belong to advanced water rescue training.
