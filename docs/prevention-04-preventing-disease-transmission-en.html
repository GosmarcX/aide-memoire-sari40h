Preventing disease transmission
The risks present, and how to protect yourself
A communicable disease spreads from one individual to another. Protecting yourself also protects the patient and the rest of the group.
1. Pincer l’extérieur du premier gant au poignet.
2. Le retirer en le retournant, sans toucher la peau.
3. Glisser deux doigts nus sous le second gant.
4. Envelopper le premier gant dans le second, puis jeter.
Figure 3 — Retrait des gants souillés, pratiqué en classe.
1
Four modes of transmission. Direct contact (blood, body fluids), indirect contact (objects, soiled dressings), airborne transmission (droplets, aerosols) and vector-borne transmission (ticks, mosquitoes, animals).
2
Universal precautions. Treat all blood and all body fluids as potentially infectious, in every patient, at all times.
3
Personal protective equipment. Gloves, pocket mask for ventilation, goggles or face shield, protective clothing. Surgical mask for droplets, N95 for aerosols.
4
Glove removal and hand washing. Remove gloves without touching their outer surface (technique covered in class), then wash your hands: it is the single most effective measure against transmission. Clean reusable equipment and dispose of soiled supplies safely.
5
Exposure and immunization. If exposed to a body fluid: wash thoroughly, document, seek medical advice promptly. Keep your immunizations current, especially tetanus, and consider rabies depending on the exposure and the region.
