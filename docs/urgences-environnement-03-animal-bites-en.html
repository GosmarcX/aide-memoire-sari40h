Animal bites
A wound, an infection risk, and the question of rabies
A bite is never trivial. Irregular, deep puncture wound from a dog or cat, crushing from a large animal, possible injury to nerves, vessels and muscles. In children, bites mostly affect the face and neck.
1
Bleeding, then pressure irrigation. Control the bleeding, assess the depth and the structures involved. Irrigate with drinking water using a syringe to create a jet, in quantity, to dislodge bacteria and debris without damaging the tissues.
2
Disinfect gently, do not close. Diluted povidone-iodine on the wound; no harsh products. A bite is a wound at high risk of infection: sterile dressing, wound left open, monitoring for infection, tetanus status checked.
3
Unknown or suspicious animal: think rabies right away. Any warm-blooded animal can carry it. A wild animal that is not afraid of humans is suspect. Never handle the animal; contact the authorities (veterinarian, animal control) if possible.
4
Washing with soap and water in the first hours reduces the risk of rabies. Then mandatory medical assessment, even if the patient has received pre-exposure doses: post-exposure vaccination is given as soon as possible. Once symptoms appear, rabies is almost always fatal.
5
Child bitten on the face or neck: immobilize the cervical spine. The mechanism (shaking, fall) justifies spinal precautions when there is significant trauma. Prevention: teach children not to provoke animals.
